is projected through a known pose and scored with NegPy's OWN warp
(keystone_matrix) and its OWN residual (skew._line_angles). The "best in family"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE TRANSFORM FAMILY IS [CODE]/[DERIVED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
logic.py:554

    a, b = converge_v * 0.005, converge_h * 0.005
    quad  = [[a, b], [1-a, -b], [1+a, 1+b], [-a, 1-b]]        # source
    dst   = [[0,0], [1,0], [1,1], [0,1]]                       # unit square

The output rect samples that quad, so the quad is what the frame becomes. Each
corner is the unit square's corner displaced by the SAME vector (a,b) — sign +1
on the main diagonal (TL, BR), −1 on the anti-diagonal (TR, BL). One displacement
vector, applied in a checkerboard pattern, is the entire model.

Consequences, exactly:

  * 2 degrees of freedom. Not 2 of a plausible 3 — 2 inside PGL(3)'s 8.
  * It IS a genuine homography (four point correspondences determine one), so
    "it is only a bilinear warp" would be the wrong criticism. It is a homography
    drawn from a 2-parameter submanifold.
  * The centre and all four edge midpoints are fixed points. The quad's top-edge
    midpoint is always exactly (0.5, 0), the bottom's always (0.5, 1). So the
    principal point is pinned to the frame centre and both mid-lines are fixed.
  * No asymmetry is representable: a frame shot off the frame centre, or a crop
    that is not centred, cannot be expressed at all.
  * |Δx| = |Δy| at every corner. The axes are not independent: raising ch drags
    the vertical vanishing point whether or not that is wanted.
  * The depth vanishing point is pinned at the principal point by construction.

Vanishing-line structure, measured on the forward map F [MEASURED]:

    cv    ch   F[2,0]     F[2,1]    vertical VP        horizontal VP
     5     0   0.000e+00  0.10526   (0.50, 10.50)      infinity
    10     0   0.000e+00  0.22222   (0.50,  5.50)      infinity
     0     5   1.053e-01  0.00000   infinity           (10.50, 0.50)
    10     6   1.461e-01  0.24039   (0.56,  5.46)      ( 8.67, 0.60)
    15    15   4.580e-01  0.45802   (0.65,  3.68)      ( 3.68, 0.65)

  * One axis alone puts its OWN vanishing point at a finite distance and the
    other's at infinity. Neither parameter can put its own VP at infinity.
  * So the family can make at most one family of lines exactly parallel, and only
    by zeroing the other parameter. With both in play — the normal case — both
    VPs stay finite and the residual is structural, not tuning.
  * Using ch at all tilts the vanishing line. At cv=10, ch=6 the horizon sits
    31° off horizontal. For a roll-0 frame the true horizon is horizontal
    [DERIVED: l = K⁻ᵀRᵀe_y has zero x-component for R = R_yR_x], so the fit has
    to burn fidelity on a horizon orientation the truth says is flat.

Against H = s·K₁·R·K₀⁻¹ [LR §3.1, §3.4], the family cannot express:

  1. Focal length. `keystone_quad` has no f. The docstring at logic.py:544-547
     says so outright: "The unit is per-cent of the frame, not tilt degrees,
     which need an enlarger magnification and focal length the pipeline does not
     model." The model still has an IMPLIED focal — the trapezoid magnitude
     fixes it — but it is one number set by cv, not an estimate. Measured
     implied f = 13397/cv px: cv=8.46 implies 1584 px (truth 1600, correct by
     luck); the deployed cv=5 implies 2680 px, 1.7x too long.
  2. K₁ ≠ K₀. The two-camera split is the whole point of Adobe's model and the
     single line the report calls out as proven to 1e-15. NegPy has no K at all,
     so it cannot make a wall parallel without forcing the pitch recovery that
     the split exists to avoid.
  3. A principal-point offset. Adobe stores UprightCenterNormX/Y ≈ 0.497/0.484;
     NegPy's is nailed to (0.5, 0.5) by the fixed midpoints.
  4. Roll from the homography. fine_rotation is a rigid rotation about the
     centre applied BEFORE the keystone (skew.py:163-171). Adobe's `Level` is
     also a rigid rotation [LR §3.4 Result 3] — but as a component of H it is a
     projective-conjugate rotation, and it does not commute with the keystone.
     NegPy's rotation is a Euclidean isometry of the plane; it cannot stand in
     for the rotational part of K₁RK₀⁻¹.
  5. Scale s. apply_keystone keeps the canvas and replicates edges
     (logic.py:584-592); the wedge is left for the user or crop_to_valid.
     Correction always costs corners and the cap therefore cannot simply be
     raised without also costing frame.
  6. An asymmetric trapezoid, hence any real off-axis keystone.

DOF: 2 (3 including the separate fine rotation). The comparison model has 8.
This is a 2-D slice, not a degraded 8-D fit — and for a flat gate it is aimed at
the right observable (§3).

"vert"/"horiz" are mean |angle off axis| in degrees; "vconv" is the report's
vertical convergence (max − min tilt) so the Lightroom figures transfer.
The gate is a rectangle in the film plane, projected through the true pose, and
the NegPy rows fit THE GATE — which is what its solver actually has available.

    original                                1.752   1.801   10.77
    exact rectification (= Guided)          0.000   0.000    0.00
    Lightroom Full [LR, ratio-matched]      0.084   0.200    0.51
    NegPy gate-fitted, no caps              0.609   0.420    0.09

    NegPy live solver, tight gate (note)    2.891   0.042   10.63
      -> returned angle -1.604, cv None, ch -4.304
      -> different gate geometry from the rows above: the gate runs past the
         canvas, so only its horizontal pair survives

  * The FAMILY is not the problem at 15°. Uncapped it leaves 0.09° of vertical
    convergence against Full's 0.51° — better than Full vertically, 2x worse
    horizontally (0.420 vs 0.200). That is the coupling of §1 showing up as
    cost: the single displacement vector is spent on verticals and drags
    horizontals with it.
  * The CAP is the problem. cv=5 clips 41 % of the correction and leaves 4.29°
    of 10.77°, i.e. 60 % removed instead of 99 %. cv hits 5 % at about 9°
    pitch (fitted cv ≈ 0.57 x pitch), so every frame past ~9° is clipped.
  * The live run is worse than either: the gate ran past the canvas, so only the
    gate's top and bottom were found, fit_v was disabled by _pair_spread
    (skew.py:230), and the solver fitted ch alone. Horizontals went 1.801 ->
    0.042 and verticals were untouched — while the -1.6° rotation pushed the
    mean vertical tilt from 1.752 to 2.891. Dropping an axis can leave that axis
    worse than doing nothing.

    pitch     5      10      15      22      30      40

  * Uncapped, the family beats Full on the vertical axis at every angle and
    keeps improving — it is a good family.
  * Capped, it is a ~5 %-of-frame corrector. At 30° it leaves 14.84° of 20.59°
    and at 40° 21.30° of 26.23°: 19-28 % removed. Past roughly 22° the
    deployed correction is close to worthless and, because the fitted rotation
    is applied anyway, the dropped axis can end up marginally worse than the
    original (30°: 3.478 vs 3.372 mean tilt).
  * Family quad vs true gate quad: 26 px at 5° (1.85 %), 165 px at 30° (11.8 %),
    267 px at 40° (19.1 %). The quad error is the floor under all of this.

Against Guided the gap is categorical: Guided solves the same 8-DOF family to
satisfy four user lines exactly and reaches 0.00/0.00 [LR]. NegPy's constraint
set is the four gate edges too, but its family is 2-DOF, so it reaches 0.61/0.42
and cannot do better at any setting.

Horizontal absolutes are metric-dependent (the report's 5.32° at scene A covers
a different detected segment set); the vertical column reproduces the report's
15.52° exactly and is the one to trust. Full's damping (0.953 / 0.932) is
calibrated to reproduce the report's 0.73°/0.36° removal ratios, not its
absolutes.

3. WHERE THE FILM-EDGE APPROACH IS GENUINELY BETTER, AND WHERE IT FAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Better — and not marginally:

  * Content independence. Four gate edges exist whether the picture is a sky, a
    white wall, a repeat pattern or a low-contrast negative. Lightroom's LSD
    needs 40+ px high-contrast segments and its Auto abstains or latches onto
    picture lines when the frame has none [LR §3.7, §6]. NegPy's fit never looks
    at the picture: "measured from the film and gate edges, never the picture"
    (skew.py:1).
  * Semantic confidence. The gates are structural, not magnitude-based:
    from_film_box, has_opposite_pair, at_canvas_edges, _SKEW_TRUSTED_CONFIDENCE
    (skew.py:303-312). They ask "did this come from the frame?" — a question
    Lightroom cannot ask, because for Lightroom the frame IS the picture. The
    report's own porting note is "do not write an abstention heuristic" [LR
    §3.7]; NegPy has one and it is the right kind.
  * Determinism and cost. Twelve Gauss-Newton steps on 2-4 lines, no RANSAC, no
    VP voting, no Powell, no non-determinism. Adobe's own `UprightDependentDigest`
    exists because its result is content-dependent [LR §7].
  * The gate is also the crop rectangle. autocrop and crop_to_valid read the same
    edges, so squaring and cropping agree. Lightroom has to crop out of the
    homography and eats 23 % of the frame for Full [LR §3.4 Result 5].
  * No patent exposure. NegPy's family is nowhere near the 2012 claims that run
    to 2033 [LR §5].
  * At <= 8° pitch and with the gate visible, the deployed result is within a
    few tenths of a degree of Lightroom Full — for a fraction of the machinery.

Fails:

  * No gate. Slide in a mount, rebate trimmed, digital crop, or a gate under the
    holder. _frame_lines falls back to _profile_lines (outermost long edges) and
    trusted_frame_skew then rejects unless at_canvas_edges (skew.py:310). It
    abstains rather than guessing from the picture — correct, but zero coverage
    on exactly the population Lightroom targets.
  * A tight scan loses the vertical pair. Measured above: the gate ran past the
    canvas, only the horizontal pair was found, cv was dropped and the result
    was worse vertically than the original. _off_border (skew.py:93-95) already
    knows about this case for film edges; the gate path does not.
  * Keystone beyond 5 %. _solve clips to ±2·_MAX_KEYSTONE and _measure_from then
    REJECTS rather than clamps if the answer is outside [_MIN_KEYSTONE,
    _MAX_KEYSTONE] (skew.py:248-252). So the axis is either clipped (41 % of
    the correction discarded at 15°) or dropped entirely. The manual sliders do
    not rescue it either: GeometryConfig caps converge_v/h at ±15 %
    (models.py:122-123), and a 30° pitch needs ~19 %.
  * _MIN_KEYSTONE = 0.3 discards any real correction below 0.3 % as "edge
    roughness, curl or lens residue" (skew.py:31), and _KEYSTONE_GAIN = 2.5
    (skew.py:33) drops the axis unless keystone beats rotation-only RMS by 2.5x.
    On a clean synthetic the rotation-only RMS is already small, so a real
    keystone has to cut the RMS by more than 2.5x to survive.
  * Axes are gated independently, so a half-fitted frame is a normal outcome
    (measured), and the surviving rotation then tilts the axis it dropped.

The honest summary: this is a corrector tuned for a copy stand that is *nearly*
square, and it is excellent there. It is not a rectifier for a frame 15° off.

4. WHAT FOCAL-BASED 3-D CORRECTION WOULD TAKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The philosophy survives intact if the new unknown is a CAMERA property rather
than a picture property. Focal length is not content: it is EXIF, or a format
prior (35 mm / 120 / 4x5 nominal), or the frame's own aspect. The detector still
sees only film and gate edges; nothing about "never the picture" changes. What
changes is that the four gate edges are allowed to constrain a pose instead of a
percent.

Rejected alternatives, for the record: fitting a general 8-DOF H to four lines
is under-determined (4 lines = 4 residuals), so a free H would need line
detection and would lose the property that makes this worth having.

Integration point — one parameterisation swap, not a rewrite:

  1. Replace the parameter vector. _solve (skew.py:183-201) already runs
     Gauss-Newton on (angle, cv, ch) against _line_angles. Change it to
     (r_x, r_y, log f): the residual, the weights, the Jacobian loop and the
     re-solve gates all stay. The rotation stays a parameter, so the horizon
     stays horizontal by construction instead of by accident.
  2. Add one prior residual on log f, sigma ≈ 0.35, from EXIF or the format's
     nominal focal. Without a prior the pitch/f pair is weakly separable at
     small angles; with it, f only moves when the gate's trapezoid insists.
  3. Add the builder. keystone_quad keeps its signature; add
     rotation_quad(r_x, r_y, f, w, h) returning the four corners of
     K R K⁻¹ applied to the frame rect, normalised, in the same corner order.
     keystone_matrix_normalized already derives everything from keystone_quad
     (logic.py:568-570) — dispatch there, and the pixel matrix and the shader
     inverse both follow for free.
  4. Replace the gates. _MAX_KEYSTONE (percent) becomes _MAX_PITCH /
     _MAX_YAW (degrees, ~25). _MIN_KEYSTONE becomes a minimum residual gain
     instead of a minimum percent. _MIN_PAIR_SEPARATION stays as is.
  5. API. GeometryConfig.converge_v/converge_h must keep working for saved
     edits, so store converge_v/h plus an optional (rotation_v, rotation_h,
     focal) triple and let the render path prefer the triple. Migration: fit the
     old cv/ch quad at a default focal and store the equivalent angles, so
     existing sidecars render identically.
  6. Parity. transform.wgsl and map_coords_to_geometry carry the quad today
     (logic.py:544-547 says to change the quad in all three places). Both need
     the 3-D path, and compute_geometry_crop_rect already assumes a projectivity,
     so it needs no change.

Two-line stopgap that buys most of §2 without any of the above: raise
_MAX_KEYSTONE from 5 to 20 and the converge_v/h slider span from ±15 to ±30.
The family was never the limit at 15° (0.09° residual uncapped, better than
Full); the cap was. The cost is corner loss, which crop_to_valid already handles,
and the horizon-tilt error of §1, which it does not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * One synthetic scene family at one focal, hard-edged fills, no grain, no lens
    distortion, no curl. Real gate edges are noisier and the detector's real
    behaviour on real rebates is not exercised here; the live-solver row is one
    crude render, not a corpus.
  * Roll is 0 throughout. NegPy's rotation is a separate stage and a rolled frame
    would change the split between the two stages.
  * The Lightroom side is the report's measured model, not a fresh run of
    Lightroom at 15°/6°; Full's damping is ratio-matched to scene A.
  * Nothing here measures crop loss or stretch for NegPy — only line residual
    and quad error.

Squarewell (ThePictureOrg) 1.6.57 — comparison and transfer attempt
====================================================================

Inspected: /Users/rohan/Downloads/ThePictureOrg-Squarewell-Mac-1.6.57.zip
Unpacked to /tmp/sq. The Lightroom plug-in ships its algorithm as readable Python
(helper/autocrop.py, helper/autocrop_detect.py, helper/bgmodel.py) plus a bundled
CPython 3.12 and rawpy. 238 MB. Licence forbids modification; irrelevant here.

1. WHAT IT IS
-------------
Auto crop and straighten for digitising slides, prints, negatives and album pages.
It finds ONE physical item on a contrasting surround and crops it away. The user
guide mentions Upright once, in passing, as something Lightroom applies before the
crop. Different problem from ours, not a worse version of it.

2. GEOMETRY: ONE DEGREE OF FREEDOM
----------------------------------
finish() (autocrop_detect.py:1365) reduces four independently measured edges to
((cx,cy),(width,height),angle) and to_lr() (:617) emits only Left/Top/Right/Bottom
plus Angle. Across the whole helper there is no warpPerspective, no
getPerspectiveTransform, and no write of Transform or PerspectiveUpright.
cv2 usage is warpAffine and minAreaRect only.

Consequence: a keystoned slide is cropped tight to its bounding rotated rectangle
and never rectified. Squarewell cannot fix converging verticals at all.

    Squarewell            1 DOF   (rotation + crop box)
    NegPy                 3 DOF   (rotation + 2 keystone)
    this port             8 DOF   (homography)
    Lightroom Upright     8 DOF + perceptual damping

3. EMPIRICAL, ON OUR MATERIAL
-----------------------------
Bundled interpreter, `autocrop.py --list ... --out ...`, defaults, --no-orient
except where noted. Angle is the straighten angle in degrees.

    lrtest/in/A_clean.tif   OK  full frame  angle 0.000000
    lrtest/in/B_strong.tif  OK  full frame  angle 0.000000
    lrtest/in/C_noisy.tif   OK  full frame  angle 0.000000
    lrtest/in/D_roll.tif    OK  full frame  angle 0.000000   <- has a known 3 deg roll
    heldout/H01.png         FAIL "no item found"
    heldout/H02.png         FAIL "no item found"

With --orient on, D_roll is given turn=3 and still angle 0.000000. So on a 3D scene
it returns identity and misses a 3 deg roll entirely; on real photographs it fails
outright.

In its own domain it is excellent. H01 pasted on black at a true 7 deg:

    OK  angle -6.989566  crop 0.184179 0.261582 0.815179 0.737388  fit 1.00/0.97

0.011 deg from truth, tight crop. The domain constraint, not the geometry, is what
it has.

5. TRANSFER ATTEMPT A — their stretch confirmation. FAILED, no change.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
They measure each edge in 16 stretches along the side and require a rise to hold
and to land on a steady level (autocrop_detect.py:689 cross(), :743 tighten()).
Ported as: sample gradient along 9 points of each segment's middle 80%, divide by
the frame's 90th-percentile gradient, drop below a threshold.

    k=0.00 0.10 0.18 0.28   all byte-identical, n=18 med 4.0843

Reason: our detector already refines on the gradient, so every emitted segment has
support. Support percentiles on a sample frame were [1%:0.718, 50%:1.498], i.e. the
weakest segment is still 72% of the reference. The filter has nothing to remove.
Their test works for them because a 1-D profile search can land on a glow or a
fabric fold; a TLS-refined segment cannot.

6. TRANSFER ATTEMPT B — length-weighted margin classification + VP re-estimation. WORSE.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
_classify assigns a whole family by unweighted majority with no margin. Made it
length-weighted with a margin, then also re-estimated each family's VP from its
merged members (which is what the oracle actually does).

    margin 0.10/0.20, weighted or not, labels only:  identical, 4.0843
    labels + VP re-estimation:  n=15  new 6.5121  base 3.8625  better 5  worse 8
                                accepted only 3

Consistent with round 12: re-splitting the detector's own VPs does not help,
because the VPs themselves are wrong. Relabelling segments around a wrong VP gives
a wrong VP.

7. WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH TAKING
--------------------------------
Not tested, and the one with real promise:

  batch consensus (autocrop_detect.py:1375 batch_adjust). Photos from one holder
  agree on where an edge sits; where most of a batch agrees, a photo whose own pick
  disagrees but which has another clear candidate near the agreed place adopts it.
  Our corpus is shoots, so the same consensus could pool family assignment or focal
  across a shoot instead of deciding per frame. Round-sized job, not a patch.

Smaller, real:

  - log2 linear light for the detector (they do this throughout; we use gray)
  - "touch": a side running off the frame is left as is instead of searched for
  - explicit per-failure reason strings, as ours already has

WHY THEY FAIL — the mechanism, not bad luck
--------------------------------------------
Attempts 2 and 8 change the *label* or the *member set* and produce identical or worse
results because the classifier's decision is almost never the thing that flips. Attempts
3, 4, 5, 7 all re-derive the vanishing points and all get worse: the port's VPs are
better than anything re-derived from the same segments, because estimate_vanishing_points
runs a multi-model RANSAC over segment pairs with a disjoint re-assignment, and every one
of my re-derivations is a cruder second guess at the same data.

The decisive observation is the acceptance count: the baseline accepts 4 of 18. The
variant attempts mostly *reduce* it. So the port is starved, not polluted. Filtering
segments removes inliers the solver needs before it can pass its own gate; the detector
already returns ~517 segments, so segment count was never the shortage.

THE REAL QUESTION
-----------------
Not "which segments are wrong" but "why does _alignment_quality reject 14 of 18 good
photos". On the 18 paired cases the candidate beats identity on 10 and loses on 8, with
medians of 4.0843 against 4.8925 — so there is a real but thin edge available, and the
gate is throwing most of it away. That is where the work belongs.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PORT
------------------------
Do not change the classifier. Keep estimate_vanishing_points and _classify as they are.
Work on the gate and on the output policy. Attempts 1-8 are all reverted; nothing in
port/ was modified.

10. THE ONE THING THAT WORKED — THE REJECTION GATE WAS FOUR DEGREES TOO TIGHT
==============================================================================
Found by asking a subagent to audit the gate rather than to improve the solver.
It tabulated all 58 rejections across 78 rows: every single row had reason 'ok',
so 100% were pure quality-gate rejections. 38 of the 58 rejected a candidate that
was BETTER than doing nothing, 33 of those by more than a whole percentage point.
Only 20 were genuinely protective, and the gate simultaneously admitted 4 fits
worse than identity. It was mis-set, not conservative.

The binding constant was limit_deg = 1.0 on the family median residual. Sweep on a
calibration/holdout split (55 and 80 photos, 25 and 27 solvable):

  limit   cal acc  cal returned   val acc  val returned
  1.0      9/25    2.4807          4/27    3.0150
  3.0     13/25    2.3504          9/27    3.0150
  4.0     15/25    2.3504         12/27    2.7455
  5.0     16/25    2.3504         16/27    2.3482
  6.0     17/25    2.3504         16/27    2.3482
  8.0     18/25    2.4807         16/27    2.3482
  all     25/25    4.9376         27/27    4.7789

Identity is 2.3504 on calibration and 3.5205 on holdout. So 5.0 degrees is neutral
on calibration and takes the holdout 33% below identity, with 12 beats against 4.
The plateau is 5.0-8.0 on both splits and accepting everything collapses to 4.78,
so the guard still does work. Shipped 5.0.

The bias guard, which the audit named as the killer, turned out to be inert: from
0.1 to 0.5 degrees changed not one decision, because the test is
`(bounds <= 0.1) | (bounds < median_deg)` and the second branch dominates once
scatter is loose. Its "2x headroom" claim was also measured only on already-accepted
rows, which is why it looked like dead weight when it was in fact the binding term.

End-to-end through solve_rows on the 22 paired photos: returned 3.4479 -> 2.6275,
better 4, worse 2, acceptance 4 -> 10. 103 tests pass; test_round10 pins the new
constant and adds a 3-degree-passes / 12-degree-fails regression.

This also explains attempts 1-8. Every one of them attacked the segment set, but the
gate was throwing away two thirds of the fits before the segment set mattered.
A tighter detector on a starved gate measures as nothing.

CONCURRENTLY CONFIRMED, same direction, not shipped
---------------------------------------------------
A robust Theil-Sen refit of the vanishing points was a wash as specified (3.2439 vs
3.1716, 7 better / 7 worse, acceptance unchanged at 4) because the angular residual
has a natural 4-8 degree spread, so a 3*median gate lands at 17.7 degrees and is
inert. But its sensitivity run with a FIXED 1.5 degree gate lifted acceptance 4 -> 10,
independently reproducing the finding above: acceptance, not residual, is what moves.

11. DIAGNOSIS — WHY SO MANY PHOTOS HAVE NO VERTICAL FAMILY
===========================================================
Delegated a pure diagnosis over 120 photos. Two independent defects, both real,
neither fixed by the obvious lever.

A. THE UNITS TRAP. estimate_vanishing_points is written in pixels: tau is floored
at 0.002*max(w,h) and clamped, and solve_ex correctly passes pixels. Called with
normalised segments instead, residuals sit near 1.4 against a gate of sqrt(3) and
a clamp of ~12, so EVERY segment is an inlier of EVERY candidate. All candidates
tie on weight, the first absorbs the whole frame (frame_inlier_frac 1.000 in
120/120), the IoU dedup rejects every later one, and exactly ONE family comes back.
is_vertical then degenerates to a frame-wide majority vote and the following line
deletes the minority orientation - a median 200 vertical segments per photo,
12,879 in total. _classify reads back only the frame-wide share, so it sees
max 0.292 and answers None. 51 of the 65 no-vertical cases are this artefact.

B. THE CANDIDATE CAP. On the correct pixel path 17 of 120 still fail, and all 17
sit exactly on MAX_VP_CANDIDATES = 9 with nine near-parallel horizontal splits.
The vertical family exists but ranks 10th-20th by weight; raising the cap to 20
recovers 14 of 17 and to 40 recovers 17 of 17.

RAISING THE CAP DOES NOT HELP THE SCORE, THOUGH. On the 22 paired photos with the
gate at 5.0: cap 9 gives returned 2.6275, cap 20 gives 3.4659, cap 40 gives 3.3184
(identity 3.4479). Acceptance rises 10 -> 12 but the median worsens, because the
recovered families are outranked for a reason and the gate then admits them. The
recovered families are size 19-71 against a typical 105-355, so they are thin.

CONCLUSION: the cap is a real ceiling on recall but not on accuracy. Keep 9. The
one thing worth shipping from this is the units warning, now in the docstring,
because the failure is silent and returns a plausible single family.

Also established: _classify's 0.5 test decides nothing at all. After the minority
deletion every family is orientation-pure, so mean(dy>dx) is exactly 0.0 or 1.0
across all 1,055 families examined and the threshold never flips. Size does not
predict the label (P(V) 0.464/0.413/0.444/0.491 by quartile); it predicts
admission. This is why attempts 1-8, which all meddled with the label decision,
measured as nothing.

12. AN ADOPTION CONTRACT ON A LABEL-FREE DEFECT ALSO FAILS
===========================================================
The port has no adoption contract: a confident wrong homography can overwrite a
good identity. The obvious guard is to compare the candidate and the identity on a
label-free measure and adopt only on a win. Built the measure as the mean
post-warp deviation of the inlier segments from the nearer image axis, and tested
margins on the 10 photos the 5.0 gate accepts:

  margin   accepted   returned   beats
  accept all   10      2.4144      7
  0.00          7      3.6464      4
  0.02          3      4.3518      1
  0.05          3      4.3518      1

Strictly monotone in the wrong direction: the more the contract rejects, the worse
it gets. So the axis-deviation defect does not track truth on this material - three
of the ten have a better defect AND a worse ground-truth residual, and the ones the
contract throws out are the good ones. With the gate at 5.0 the correct policy is
simply to accept. That is now six independent label-free statistics that fail to
discriminate: gradient support, length margin, post-warp axis deviation,
endpoint concentration, span coverage and cross-strip step.

13. THE GATE FIX HOLDS IN BOTH MODES
====================================
Section 10 measured mode 'full'. Re-measured with mode 'auto', the mode a user
actually gets:

  mode   new      old      identity   better  worse  accepted
  auto   1.4562   1.8087   1.6949     4       2      10
  full   2.6275   3.4479   3.4479     4       2      10

Both modes improve on their own old value and both now sit below identity, with the
same 4-better / 2-worse and the same acceptance of 10. The effect is not

14. PEEL ASSIGNMENT — NULL, AND IT NAMES THE REAL CEILING
========================================================
Escalating tolerance with consumption (take each family at the tightest tolerance
where it is clean, remove its members, then relax for the next family) so one family
cannot absorb another's segments. Ten schedules tested, including vertical-first
ordering and pure consumption.

Best case a wash: 10 deg start / x1.5 gives 2.4714 against 2.4807, 4 better 6 worse,
5 accepted against 4. Worst case a catastrophic outlier (6736916, 2.04 -> 48.6 at a
5 deg start). The mandated 0.5 deg start and 1.0 deg start look better in absolute
median (3.6066, 3.8327) but that is selection - they fail to fit 9 and 8 of the 18,
and the cases they keep are the easy ones, so their paired baseline on the survivors
is only 1.6546 and 2.0676 with 1 accepted.

THE ROOT CAUSE, MEASURED, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------
Our families are not parallel line sets, they are convergent bundles. The angular
spread of a family about its own median direction has a median of 5-30 degrees
(observed 12.9, 17.4, 35.8, 4.2), while the RANSAC inlier gate is 1.7 PIXELS. A
0.5 or 1.0 degree window keeps 2-5 of 115-227 members, and the 3-inlier floor then
kills the rest of the frame. The film app's tight start works because its sides are
genuinely parallel; a vanishing-point family converges, so it needs 5-10 degrees
before the tolerance means anything.

Any future tolerance work must start at 5-10 degrees and prune only the three
families the solver actually uses. Peel re-partitions the same families and never
touches family selection, which the 0.8353 oracle says is the ceiling.

How Lightroom's Upright really works

We drove Lightroom Classic through its own solver on scenes where we knew the exact camera rotation, then read what it wrote into the catalogue. Upright ships two things you would not guess: a rotation projected through a second, different camera, and a correction that deliberately stops short. Both are measured here, in the binary and in the behaviour.

01 — THE ANSWER

Is it unique? Yes, but not in the geometry

Every geometric part of Upright is off-the-shelf. It finds lines, votes on vanishing points (where parallel lines appear to meet), builds a Manhattan frame (three directions at right angles, taken from the scene), and runs a direct search over candidate rotations. darktable's ashift does the same kind of thing.

Two things are not off-the-shelf, and neither is in the geometry. One is an algebra detail almost everyone gets wrong. The other is the behaviour.

The most important line here. The transform Lightroom ships is H = s · K₁ · R · K₀⁻¹: a rotation re-projected through a different camera. Not K·R·K⁻¹. Almost everyone who tries this guesses the shared-K form, and it is wrong by 32%. We fitted Adobe's own stored transforms to both. Shared K stalls at a leftover error of 3.19e-01. Letting the two focal lengths and both principal points (where the lens axis meets the sensor) float reaches 5.65e-16, which is machine precision, fifteen digits.

That is why Upright looks natural. Two independent sets of camera settings let Adobe make a wall parallel without also forcing the perspective recovery a physically correct rotation would demand. It is a projection, not a camera move.

The second unique thing is behavioural. Auto refuses to fully straighten, and how far it stops short is a smooth, measured function of how far the camera was tilted. It is a compromise tuned to what looks right.

02 — THE MODEL

What it writes

Every Upright mode writes one 3×3 homography (a warp that keeps straight lines straight), row-major, in normalised frame coordinates, into UprightTransform_N inside Adobe_imageDevelopSettings.text. Six slots come out of a single solver pass, so one click yields every mode at once:

44Verticalkills pitch and yaw (yaw skews verticals too)
55Guideduser-drawn edges; the one path with no corpus evidence

The stored integer is the slot index. The decomposition:

H = s · K₁ · R · K₀⁻¹ K₀ = camera settings of the source camera (focal estimated per image) K₁ = camera settings of the destination camera, distinct from K₀ R = the rotation that answers the perceptual energy (how good the result looks to a person) s = leftover scale factor

Focal length (how wide the view is) really is estimated per image rather than pinned to a default: UprightFocalLength35mm came back 38.97 / 40.00 / 39.79 / 39.87 mm against a true 40.24 mm, under 3.2% error and mutually consistent. The conversion uses a full-frame diagonal, f35 × hypot(W,H) / hypot(24,36), with hypot(24,36) = 43.2666 computed at runtime. The obvious /36 form is only right at 3:2. These scenes are 7:5, so it would read 2.25% low.

03 — THE DAMPING

Why it refuses to straighten, measured

The CVPR 2012 paper (Automatic Upright Adjustment of Photographs, Lee et al., DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2012.6247761) describes per-axis weights, λ(θ) = λ₀·exp(−θ²/2σ²), with σ_v = π/12 and σ_h = π/15. Those constants are real and present in the shipping binary as float32 literals. Both the squared and the reciprocal-squared forms appear, which are the two you need to evaluate the exponential:

literal found in CameraRawvalueequals
σ_v²0.0685389(π/12)²
1/(2σ_v²)7.29512521/(2·(π/12)²)
σ_h²0.0438649(π/15)²
1/(2σ_h²)11.39863321/(2·(π/15)²)

Measuring the behaviour on its own agrees. Twenty-nine controlled solves give a yaw decay of σ_yaw = 11.64° against the paper's σ_h = 12.00°, a 3.0% match. The vertical constant is the awkward one. Measured over 80 images it is unit-dependent: 13.33° [9.94, 22.72] in visible-keystone units, 22.26° [16.67, 39.58] in camera-pitch units. The literal 15.00° falls inside the first range and outside the second. The binary stores a bare radian with no note of which axis it belongs to.

The surprise: Auto needs pitch to do anything measured

On a pure-yaw frame, with verticals already parallel and horizontals converging, Auto does nothing. It moves horizontal convergence by 0.01° while Full removes 96%. Add any pitch and the gate opens:

Auto is driven by verticals. It asks whether verticals need fixing, and if they are already parallel it does nothing at all. Once it decides to act, it drags the horizontal family along. No single θ = total tilt expresses this, because the two weights key off different axes.

Roll gets a third rule and is essentially always applied: median Auto/Full roll ratio 0.928 across 12,883 real photos.

04 — WHAT IT DOES TO A PHOTO

Measured against known truth

A synthetic Manhattan scene, true camera pitch 22°, yaw 6°, verticals converging 15.52°:

modevertical conv.horizontal conv.p99 stretchcrop kept
original15.52°5.32°1.000100%
Auto5.35°0.73°1.111100.0%
Full0.73°0.36°1.48677.1%
Vertical0.73°5.96°1.53770.2%

Auto removes 65% of the vertical convergence and 86% of the horizontal. It applies a 1.11× stretch and crops nothing at all. Full does straighten the frame, and pays 23% of the frame and 1.49× distortion for it.

Abstention means "nothing to correct", not a veto. 9.7% of photos carrying both Auto and Full data get identity in every slot, and in 100% of them Full is identity too. The solver decides once per photo and all six slots inherit it. A 17-image probe (noise, blur, contrast, crop) produced zero abstentions and a real focal every time. The abstainers are already upright. There is a mechanism behind it: the solver enforces a hard minimum of two distinct vanishing points, and a winning VP must beat its runner-up by a score ratio of ≤0.75 and a gap of >1.5. Straight photos fail both tests for the same reason. Emit identity when the correction falls below the noise, and bail below two VPs.

It does not need a Manhattan scene either. Single walls, railings, three-plane corners and curved content all correct to 0.83–0.96 of ideal at the same pose. Curves are the best protected family: a 7 m arc goes 1.00 → 0.34 px circle-fit rms and the fitted radius stays at 360 against a true 350, so the arc is not straightened. That protection is a side effect of the global warp. No term in the energy knows about arcs. The real defect is elsewhere: the focal solve runs 15–16% low on three families, which is what leaves the ~1.1% aspect error.

PerspectiveScale is 100.0 on every photo and the global Transform sliders are always exactly 0. All of Upright's effect lives in UprightTransform_N. The crop follows from the homography; there is no separate crop stage to copy.

Guided: the strongest mode, and the one to port first measured

Guided is driven by up to four edges the user draws. Its storage format came out of Lightroom's own compiled Lua: UprightFourSegments_0..3, each "x1,y1,x2,y2" at nine decimal places in normalised frame coordinates, plus UprightFourSegmentsCount.

Writing the guides does not run the solver, and we now know why. A stored MD5 digest blocks re-analysis: while the digest still matches, the solve is skipped. Settings writes land in the serialised record rather than going through the panel, so the digest never goes stale. Overwrite it with 32 zeros and the next re-analysis fires, and then a single Update click solves with no line drawing at all. We reproduced a hand-drawn transform on a fresh photo from the stored segments alone to confirm it.

It also needs exactly four. Three segments with a count of four writes nothing. Four segments with a count of eight is worse: the guides are thrown away, slot 5 stays identity, and an ordinary unguided solve lands in slots 1–4 with a focal, so it looks like it worked. Assert the count and all four keys, then check slot 5 is not identity.

The geometry of the guides matters as much as the count. One contradictory guide among three good ones is a hard failure that writes nothing, three runs out of three, and so is four segments all collinear on the horizon. Two near-parallel verticals are worse than either: the solve succeeds, fits the guides to exactly 0.000°, and under-corrects to 38% of the reference. Zero leftover error on your own guides tells you nothing for degenerate input, so check the size of the correction against a normal result too. An absent slot 5 means the solve ran and failed; an identity one means it never ran.

Guided is not additive. It wipes slots 1–4 to nil, so an earlier Auto or Full result is destroyed. Capture it first if you want both.

Guided does not use the perceptual objective at all. It takes the user's lines as hard constraints and solves the same s·K₁·R·K₀⁻¹ family to satisfy them exactly. No energy model, no line detector, no weights, no abstention rule. Just the algebra and a solver. If you port one mode, port this one.

05 — NOVELTY AUDIT

Off-the-shelf against Adobe-specific

Off-the-shelf, anyone can do this

  • bespoke a-contrario line extraction (lines scored against what random noise would produce) AGPL/Apache
  • per-segment line-dual candidates, ≥3 votes, ≥2 VPs, 0.75-ratio / 1.5-gap margin
  • Manhattan frame from three orthogonal VPs
  • Powell / direct search: try candidates, keep the best

Adobe-specific

  • H = s·K₁·R·K₀⁻¹, two sets of camera settings, proven to 1e-15 measured
  • adaptive λ per axis with a pitch gate measured
  • joint estimate_MKR rather than sequential paper
  • eye-line term, normalised by line weight
  • Perkins's-law junction inequality plus a log barrier
  • Jacobian penalty on curved edges
  • focal from metadata, no prior weight

The novelty sits in the objective function. A conventional "detect VPs, push them to infinity" implementation is Full with the safety rails removed.

The orthogonality test is weaker than it looks, though. A red-team pass reproduced the leftover error (2.16e-16, n=400) and showed it is mostly an artefact of so many free parameters: six camera settings against five independent equations leaves a free direction, so ten random but realistic perspective warps also fit at 1e-16. What survives is narrower and real. The transforms disagree (≈0.10) with every single-focal form we tested, so two focal lengths are a genuine algebraic requirement. What dies is whether the values can be pinned down: the stored camera settings fit exactly for only 14.7% of photos and the source focal is free over 0.25×–4×. Use them as a hint, not a substitute.

Patent family, priority 2012-04-27, active to 2033: US 9,008,460 · 9,582,855 · 9,098,885 · 9,519,954 · 9,727,787. Not 9,088,885, which is a telecom patent about mobile-number registration.

Two things the claims do not say. The adaptive weights are unclaimed: σ_v and σ_h appear in the paper and in all five specifications, and in none of the 100 claims, so changing or dropping them gets you around nothing. The VP ordering is covered only by what it does, so a different detector does not help either. What the claims cover is behaviour, "homography … computed at least in part according to one or more human perception-based criteria". That is Auto, described exactly.

That is why the build order flips. "user", "guide" and "guide line" appear in none of the 100 claims. Guided clears four of the five claim 1s on its face and scores better than Full anyway. Build Guided first, ship Auto last. The one gap left in the escape route is US 9,582,855 c11, a reprojection-free "planar transformation … according to human perception-based criteria". Keep the guided solve purely algebraic and never call it perceptual.

The gap in that argument, stated plainly. US 9,582,855 claim 1 is the broadest member and the last to expire, and it carries the same limitation about human perception-based criteria. Guided escapes the other four claim 1s, so a Guided-only product has a position worth arguing. A product that ships Auto does not.

06 — THE PORT

What we built, and how far it gets

A recovered model is only worth something if it can be rebuilt from. The port is that test, and it is where the work is least finished.

Two separate results: accurate on synthetic scenes, not usable on real photographs yet.

On four synthetic scenes the port reproduces Adobe closely, but that number is mostly output policy, not geometry. Opt-in framing and scaling cut the raw difference from 33.27% to 0.587%. Give the same policy an oracle geometry and it scores 0.598%, so the solver adds about 2% and the policy about 98%. Both figures are in-sample. The 0.587 is a framing=True, scaling=True number: benchmark_adobe.py calls solve_ex bare and reports the ~31% unframed path, so never compare the two. It also predates round 13.

On 450 real photographs it accepts 80 of 900 attempts (8.9%, 40 photos) and returns identity on the rest. It beats naive straightening 56.2% of the time and loses to plain identity 40.7% of the time (medians 2.20% / 3.35% / 2.02%). Rounds 11 and 12 showed the bottleneck is choosing which lines belong to which direction: an oracle selector cuts Full median 3.66% to 0.93%, and a full VP/Mahalanobis-style selector closed none of it (3.6638% → 3.6720%). Round 12 shipped nothing and production was restored byte for byte.

Two review defects are fixed, and so is the Auto over-correction round 5 failed on. The response is hump-shaped in pitch, not monotone, and all four scenes sat at 22° and 34°, the region where the port was already right. Measured from 12,075 real Adobe pairs, Auto mean drops 32.35% → 27.76% and B_strong 42.37% → 24.08%, meeting a target open since round 4, at the cost of a 2% D_roll regression from missing roll coupling.

Still open: the family-selection gap above, F4–F13 from the fuzz pass, a 154-line-stale backup path and a never-written TSV header in OPERATIONS.md, and port_status.txt / port_review.txt, which still call the fixed seed-4 failure the top open defect. Fixed from the fuzz pass: F1 (horizon crossing), F2 (1×1 frames), F3 (wide-frame classification) and the 45° divide-by-zero. 102 tests pass.

The port is MIT at research/upright/port/. It implements the recovered model and the damping curve, and its constants carry provenance tags: PROVEN where the binary shows it, MEASURED where a corpus fitted it, CHOICE where we made it up. Three of the last kind exist and the README says so: the Cauchy loss scale, the quality-gate form, and the scale switch.

Loose ends, and how they closed or did not

questionoutcome
Two pure-yaw scenes (Y06, Y15) where even Full failed Closed. Both reproduce exactly, so they are deterministic, not noise. Two distinct modes: Y06 silently abstains (near-identity in every mode), Y15 applies a large warp that misses its target (14.66° → 14.55°).
Which camera do the stored camera settings describe? Closed. UprightFocalLength35mm tracks the true focal to 3.8% median across 29 scenes with one shared true focal. They are the source camera, K₀, and the ambiguity collapses.
Guided mode, previously unanalysed Closed. Format recovered, trigger isolated, behaviour measured. Strictly the strongest mode and the cheapest to port.
Abstention trigger (9.7% of photos) Closed. Not a veto. Full is identity in 100% of abstainers, so the decision is global and means "nothing to correct". The abstainers are already-upright photos.
Is the 0.587% a solver result? No. The same framing+scale policy applied to oracle geometry scores 0.598% against the port's 0.587%, so 98% of the reduction is output policy and 2% solver. Both are in-sample: the 10/90 blend and the 1.10 switch were fitted on the scored slots.
Did the round-12 selector close the oracle gap? No. Full median 3.6638% → 3.6720%, gap closed −0.30%. Purity, coverage and horizon failures improved, but Full p90 worsened and a new false accept appeared on the abstention control, so nothing shipped.
Does non-Manhattan content break the port? No. All four non-Manhattan families correct as well as the Manhattan control in Full at 15°/6°. The failures are pose-driven: Auto collapses above 25° pitch/yaw, and Full breaks on floors at pitch ≥40° with a wide lens (40° → 52.5°, worse than identity).
Did the round-5 Auto over-correction ever get fixed? Yes, in round 13, at the second attempt, by changing the data. Rounds 5 and 6 failed because all four scenes sat at 22°/34°, where the port was already right. Adobe's own response from 12,075 real pairs is hump-shaped (peak ~0.80 at 6–12°, ~0 above 35°), so the monotone form became a measured 12-knot table: Auto mean 32.35% → 27.76%, B_strong 42.37% → 24.08% (target met), D_roll +0.58 (2%) from missing roll coupling.
Can a solved H be unusable while ok=True? Yes, F1, now fixed. The corner-w test existed in frame_output but was not applied unless framing=True; 34 of 82 ok results in the fuzz matrix crossed the projective horizon. solve_ex now rejects them unconditionally. F3 (pixel-space classification on 20:1 frames), the 45° divide-by-zero and F2 (1×1 now returns insufficient line structure) are fixed too. F4–F13 are open.
Why does the benchmark say ~31% when the report says 0.587%? Different paths. benchmark_adobe.py calls solve_ex with no framing or scaling and measures the unframed path; 0.587 is framing=True, scaling=True. Never compare the two, and note 0.587 predates round 13.
08 — THE OTHER TOOL

How NegPy squares a scan

NegPy solves a different problem and should not be judged as a worse Upright. Upright assumes a camera pointed at a 3D scene; NegPy's skew.py assumes flat film photographed on a copy stand, where the only distortions are the negative's rotation in the carrier and the easel's tilt. It reads the film and gate edges and never the picture, fits three parameters (rotation plus two keystone values) instead of a rotation and a focal, and declines to keystone unless the gain beats rotation-only by 2.5×, which is the same instinct as Lightroom's abstention, earned with a leftover-error test. Its keystone_quad is a two-degree-of-freedom slice of the projective group's eight, so the principal point is nailed to the frame centre and it can make at most one line family parallel. That family is not the limit, though: uncapped it beats Lightroom Full vertically at every angle from 5° to 40°. The 5% cap is what holds it back, and two lines, _MAX_KEYSTONE 5 → 20 and the manual sliders ±15% → ±30%, buy most of the difference. The honest analogue for NegPy is Guided, not Full, since both are constraint-driven. Only Guided's constraint set reaches zero.

REAL-PHOTOGRAPH EVALUATION OF THE UPRIGHT PORT
==============================================

Date: 2026-10-28. Read-only catalog access (`mode=ro`, `PRAGMA query_only=ON`).
Port source not edited. All numbers in this file are reproducible from the
artifacts named at the bottom.

HEADLINE
--------
On real photographs the port does not return a wrong homography. It returns
*no* homography. The policy that was live when this evaluation started rejected
**600 of 600** records (300 photos x 2 modes) and returned exact identity every
time. Its real-photo error is therefore not a residual, it is the size of
Adobe's own correction: median **2.35%** of the diagonal, p99 **19.47%**, max
**28.28%**, with **23.0%** of records above 5%. The synthetic mean was 0.587%.
Real-photo performance is roughly 4x worse, and the reason is abstention, not
geometry.

A sibling round rewrote the gate (`_alignment_quality`) at 08:50 while this run
was in flight. Under that newer policy the port accepts 9.7% of records and is
genuinely better than identity *there*, but the whole-sample median barely
moves (2.35% -> 2.17%). Both policies are reported. The newer one is not a fix
for the headline, it is a 10% dent in it.

SCORING
-------
RMS Euclidean distance between the port's and Adobe's mapped positions over the
fixed 25x25 source grid used by `benchmark_adobe.agreement` (625 points,
including all four corners), expressed as percent of the source diagonal.
Catalog maps are in sensor axes; every candidate is converted through the
orientation matrix before scoring, so both sides share one gauge. Translation
and uniform scale are *not* factored out — Adobe's framing is part of the truth.

`frame_output` then `scale_output` are applied in that order for every
"framing+scale" arm. Where the solve returns identity the two helpers are no-ops.

Some gate-bypassed outputs are geometrically invalid: the homography places part
of the source frame on the projective horizon, so the sample grid cannot be
mapped. Those records are counted separately as *unscorable* and excluded from
the percentile tables. Every table's denominator is its scorable count, given as
n. The shipped path is always scorable because identity is.

SAMPLE
------
Frozen at `evidence/round9_sample.json`: 300 photos drawn with
`random.Random(9009)` from the 5,443 catalog photos that have a non-identity
transform in **both** slot 1 (Auto) and slot 2 (Full) and whose file resolves
through `Adobe_images -> AgLibraryFile` and exists on disk.

    Upright-analysed rows            14,709
    both slots non-identity          13,287   (90.3% of analysed photos)
    file missing on disk              7,719   (58.1% of those — sample drawn from survivors)
    eligible and on disk              5,443
    sampled                             300

All 300 decoded; no missing files. 274/300 are 3:2. Focal 35 mm-equivalent:
p5 26 mm, median 35.1 mm, p95 55 mm, max 137.6 mm. Minimum short side 4000 px.
**The sample contains no extreme aspect ratios and no tiny images**, so those
two candidate tail explanations are untested here; see Limitations.

Every photo is scored in both modes, so n = 600 records unless stated. Per-photo
figures take the worse of the two modes.

    percent of diagonal   2.166   8.340  19.474   28.275   416    132      4

On the 58 accepted records the port is worth having: median 0.815% against
identity's 1.972% on the same records, beating identity in 39/58 (67.2%), and
only 1/58 above 5%. But 58 records is 9.7% of the sample, and the other 90.3%
still contribute their full Adobe correction. That is why the sample median
falls only from 2.345% to 2.166%.

CONTROL 1 — FRAMING+SCALE DISABLED
----------------------------------
On policy A this control is *exactly* identical to the shipped path: 0 of 600
records differ, because the solve returns identity and both helpers are no-ops.
Framing+scale cannot be evaluated through the shipped gate at all.

On policy B, restricted to the 58 records the gate accepts, the difference is
large and one-directional:

    shipped (framing+scale on)   p50  0.815   p90  2.940   max   5.784
    framing+scale disabled       p50 10.129   p90 51.877   max 125.164
                                 >5% 46/58   >20% 18/58

Framing and scaling are the single largest contributor to agreement on real
photos — a 12x median difference. The raw solve output is systematically
mis-translated and mis-scaled against Adobe's framing, and the two helpers
absorb nearly all of it. Any evaluation that disables them and reports the raw
solve is measuring the wrong thing.

CONTROL 2 — NAIVE FULL RECTIFICATION
------------------------------------
`_base_solve(..., shared_k=True, mode="full")`, i.e. the textbook single-camera
`K R K^-1` on the same detected segments and the same focal prior, no gate, full
damping. Scored on the same grid, with and without framing+scale:

    naive, raw              p50 14.465  p90 103.881  p99 1031.501  max 1219.621
                            >1% 442/488  >5% 343/488  >20% 203/488
    naive + framing+scale   p50  3.148  p90  17.019  p99   40.049  max   48.229
                            >1% 388/488  >5% 179/488  >20%  40/488

    gate bypassed, raw              p50  9.510  p90  69.667  p99 602.648  max 1219.621
    gate bypassed + framing+scale   p50  2.571  p90  14.821  p99  43.275  max  55.368

Paired on the 329 records where both produce a mapping:

    port beats naive                 185/329  (56.2%)
      Auto mode                       86.1% win, p50 1.44 vs 3.47
      Full mode                       25.8% win, p50 2.89 vs 3.00
    port beats identity              134/329  (40.7%)
    naive beats identity             104/329  (31.6%)
    median, same records             port 2.201 | naive 3.350 | identity 2.023

**Is the port better on real photos?** Against naive full rectification, yes —
clearly, and almost entirely because of Auto mode and the far better p90 (14.8%
vs 17.0%). Against doing nothing, no: the port loses to identity on the median
and on the win rate. In Full mode the port beats identity only 39.3% of the time
and actually loses to naive (25.8% win); every bit of the port's advantage over
naive is in Auto, where it wins 86.1%. Identity is a strong baseline on real photographs because
Adobe's own corrections are usually small, and the port's residual is the same
size as the correction it is trying to make.

The port is a better *naive rectifier*. It is not yet a better *correction* than
identity on an unconditional basis. Only inside the gate does it clear identity.

TAIL ANALYSIS — THE >5% CASES
-----------------------------
Two different tails live here and they must not be conflated.

(a) Tail of the shipped port = tail of Adobe's correction. It is not a port
failure, but it is what the caller experiences. Under policy A: 138/600 records
and 116/300 photos. Concentrated, not random:

    mode        Full 115/300 (38.3%)     Auto 23/300 (7.7%)
    orientation BC   48/152 (31.6%)      AB 49/278 (17.6%)   DA 39/160 (24.4%)
    portrait         92/346 (26.6%)      landscape 46/254 (18.1%)
    focal        <=50mm 137/562 (24.4%)  >50mm 1/38 (2.6%)

    All four >20% records are 35-40 mm and 24-60 MP; three of the four are Full
    mode and three are DA (portrait) orientation: DSC07555.ARW 28.27%,
    R0000497.DNG 24.10%, R0000477.DNG 22.80% in both modes. Portrait + Full +
    normal-to-wide lens is the worst combination in the sample.

    Rule of thumb: the shipped error is large exactly where the photo needed a
    large correction and the port gave none. Long lenses are safe; portrait
    orientation and Full mode are not.

(b) Tail of the port's own geometry, gate bypassed. 174/543 scorable records
>5%, 39/543 >20%, max 55.4%, plus the 12.8% that do not score at all. This one
is strongly predictable and monotone in two solved quantities:

    |solved pitch|   n     p50     p90     max   >5%
      <=2 deg       86    0.81    5.16   24.60     9
      2-5           80    1.08    7.67   47.03    12
      5-10          78    2.44   12.76   54.24    23
      10-20         45    4.40   13.76   21.47    19
      >20           98    7.65   29.35   55.37    64  (65%)

    keystone        n     p50     p90           >5%
      <=10 deg      36    0.83    8.97            5
      10-20         77    1.10    6.97           12
      20-35        192    2.50   11.91           56
      35-60         40   10.80   32.78           29  (72%)
      >60           42    6.66   22.88           25  (60%)

    invalid output — mapping crosses the projective horizon, so the scoring
    grid cannot be evaluated at all: **57/444 solved records (12.8%)** for the
    port, against 112/444 (25.2%) for naive. These are hard failures, not
    residuals, and they are excluded from every distribution above. Where the
    quad merely degenerates to near-zero area (< 0.2) it still scores, and all
    15 such records are >5% with p50 26.3%.

    **12.2% of solves (54/444) pin pitch on the hard +/-1.2 rad bound** and
    return exactly 68.75 deg; 21 of the 32 that still score are >5%. Pitch p50 is
    7.5 deg and p90 is 68.75 deg, so the bound is not a rare safety valve on real
    photos — it is a routine operating point. Keystone is similarly saturated:
    p50 21.5 deg, p99 85.7 deg, against synthetic scenes that sit far lower.

    Not predictive: segment count (>=600 segments p50 4.53%, <600 p50 2.35% — more
    lines is *worse*), and calibration acceptance (accepted 36/134 >5%, rejected
    138/409 — a wash).

So yes, the tail is predictable, but the useful predictors are solved-quantity
magnitudes (pitch, keystone, quad area), which are available after the solve and
are exactly what a gate should consume. A rule "abstain when |pitch| > 20 deg or
keystone > 35 deg" would remove most of the intrinsic tail. The current gate
does not use either.

GATE CALIBRATION
----------------
The gate statistic works; the threshold is mis-scaled by roughly 20x. Under
policy A, `family_median_deg` has Spearman +0.615 against the ungated error and
AUC 0.831 for predicting >5%; `family_bias_bound_deg` gives +0.606 / 0.827. The
acceptance limit was 0.114 deg at p50 while the observed scatter p50 was 2.18
deg — 19x over. Sweeping the bias bound on policy A:

    threshold   accepted   port p50   port p90   identity p50   port<identity
    0.11           0          —          —           —              —
    0.50          68         0.73       3.61        2.08            70.6%
    1.00         136         0.85       3.02        1.56            63.2%
    2.00         214         1.21       5.28        1.56            50.5%
    none         387         2.54      17.29        2.07            38.0%

The statistic separates the two populations cleanly: bias bound p50 2.41 deg on
photos Adobe corrects versus 25.0 deg on photos Adobe abstains. Threshold 1.0
deg accepts 30.6% of the corrected set and 0/456 abstention records. Policy B's
1.0 deg scatter cap plus "bias below its own scatter" lands in the same region
and does reach 58 accepts with zero false positives.

ABSTENTION
----------
Adobe abstains on 1,422 of 14,709 analysed photos (9.67%), both slots identity.
That matches the ~9.7% figure. Because the main sample was filtered to
non-identity, **0/300 of the main sample are Adobe abstentions** — a deliberate
consequence of the sampling rule, so the main table says nothing about them.

A separate control of 229 such photos (456 records, all files verified) answers
it directly:

    policy A shipped: 456/456 return exact identity, error 0.0000%, 0/456 >1%
    policy B shipped: 456/456 return exact identity, 0/456 accepted

The port matches Adobe's abstention **perfectly on both policies, with zero
false positives**. That is partly structural: 208/456 records (45.6%) fail before
the gate even runs (206 with no VP pair, 2 with under four segments).

But it is also not that the port *would* abstain if allowed to speak. 248 of the
456 records do produce a solve; 152 of those survive framing and scale, and of
those **151 (99.3%) land >1% from identity and 137 (90.1%) land >5%**, median
14.26%. The errors are not marginally over threshold, they are large. On
essentially every Adobe abstention the port would have invented a serious
correction. The gate is doing real work here, and any loosening must keep the
1.0 deg separation to preserve it.

VERDICT
-------
* The shipped real-photo error is **4x the synthetic 0.587%** and it is 100%
  abstention. Nothing in the synthetic suite exercised this, because the
  synthetic gate passed on every fixture. (Strictly, 2.345% is a median and
  0.587% is the round-10 synthetic mean, so the ratio is indicative, not exact;
  the synthetic p90 was 2.21% against a real p90 of 8.40%, which is the same
  story with matching statistics.)
* Framing+scale is not a nicety. It is the difference between 0.815% and 10.1%
  median on the records where the port speaks, and it is doing far more work
  than the solver. The README's "not validated on real photos" note for
  `scale_output` now has a number: it is the most valuable component measured
  here.
* The port beats naive full rectification (56.2% paired, p90 14.8 vs 17.0) but
  loses to identity (40.7% paired win rate). All of the advantage is Auto
  (86.1% win over naive); in Full mode the port wins only 25.8% against naive.
* The tail is predictable — pitch, keystone, quad area — and the bound at
  68.75 deg is being hit on 12.2% of solves. The gate ignores all three.
* Policy B is a real improvement in the accepted region (0.815% median, 67.2%
  wins over identity, 0 false positives on 456 abstention records) but covers
  only 9.7% of records, so the unconditional median is still 2.17% and the
  headline stands.

LIMITATIONS
-----------
* Sample is 3:2-dominated (274/300), no extreme aspect, no tiny images, 99
  distinct focals but only 38 records >50 mm. "Extreme aspect" and "tiny image"
  remain untested tail hypotheses; they are not ruled out.
* 58% of the eligible catalog photos are missing on disk, so the sample is the
  surviving subset of one external SSD, not a random sample of the library.
* The naive control reuses the port's detector, VPs and focal prior, differing
  only in the camera model and damping. It is a model ablation, not an
  independent implementation.
* The gate thresholds in the calibration sweep were chosen on this sample; the
  1.0 deg figure is not cross-validated. A sibling round is running a
  calibration/validation split for that.
* 300 photos, one library, one machine, one day. The port was edited twice
  underneath this run; both policies are named by digest above.

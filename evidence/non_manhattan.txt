NON-MANHATTAN SCENES — nothing breaks; the arcs are not straightened
(supersedes the partial version of 22:35; the arc gap it flagged is now closed
and err_gt is replaced by an interpretable metric)

BOTTOM LINE
  All four non-Manhattan families are corrected to the same standard as a
  Manhattan four-plane corner at the identical pose, in both modes. No family
  degenerates, none is wrong. The known arc is not bent: its circle-fit rms
  falls 3x (1.00 -> 0.34 px) and its conic aspect stays at 1.009-1.011 against
  1.000 ideal, i.e. every bit of deformation is the global warp, none is
  arc-specific — there is no straightness prior pulling the curve flat.
  The one real defect, present in every family including the Manhattan control,
  is focal estimation: 33.9-39.8 mm solved against a true 41.14 mm, leaving a
  ~1.1% aspect error. The one structural gap is unavoidable geometry — a single
  frontal wall carries almost no yaw information — and even there Adobe
  recovers yaw to 0.4 deg rather than failing.

HARNESS (working, plus two corrections to the recipe)
  Trigger: activate Lightroom, keystroke "1" with {command down, option down},
  then AXPress the item containing "Import Sweep".
  CORRECTION 1: the plugin items are NOT nested under a submenu in the AX tree.
  "Upright Probe" is a plain sibling header and "Import Sweep" is a direct child
  of `menu 1 of menu item "Plug-in Extras"`. `menu 1 of menu item "Upright
  Probe"` raises "Invalid index (-1719)" open or closed; iterate the flat list.
  Two `key code 53` escapes and a 2 s settle before the keystroke are required,
  otherwise `menu bar item "Library"` itself fails to resolve (-1728).
  CORRECTION 2: a second agent owns lrplugin/.../ImportSweep.lua and rewrites
  it. A separate plugin (uprightprobe_nm.lrdevplugin) was installed, but
  Lightroom only rescans the Modules folder at startup, so the shared file had
  to be swapped in per run and restored after. Two concurrent plugin tasks also
  fight over the active source, so runs were serialised.

METHOD
  make_test_images.render_scene(name, rx, ry, rz), 1400x1000, f=1600, pitch
  15 deg, yaw 6 deg, rz=0:
    (a) NM_single_wall  one plane filling the frame, open on the other side;
                        verticals + in-plane horizontals only
    (b) NM_railing      the same wall plus railings running into depth, which
                        restores the second vanishing point
    (c) NM_curves       two 7 m / 5 m arcs on the wall and a 3.2 m cylindrical
                        column; no straight edges among the curves
    (d) NM_three_plane  back wall + side wall + floor
  Controls: CT_manhattan2, a four-plane Manhattan box at the same 15/6 pose;
  A_clean (22/6) and B_strong (34/12) at the old poses. The first control
  attempt was byte-identical to NM_three_plane (its fourth wall fell outside
  the frame); the box was narrowed until it rendered distinctly.
  Each photo: PerspectiveUpright=0, then 1 (Auto), then 2 (Full), 10 s / 12 s
  settles. Read back from the catalog mode=ro, Adobe_imageDevelopSettings,
  keys UprightTransform_1/2 and UprightFocalLength35mm.

METRICS (convention free — the stored transform is not exactly K R K^-1)
  mag    max|H-I|, H rescaled to H[2,2]=1
  f35    solved focal in 35 mm equivalent; truth for this render is 41.14
  vdev   median |angle from vertical| of the known vertical 3-D lines, deg,
         over the in-frame part of each line only (the earlier 22:35 note used
         a 300 px extrapolation and so reported ~4.0 deg where the in-frame
         value is 3.3-4.3; both directions improve, so the conclusion holds)
  hdev   same against horizontal
  sim    max px distance between the Adobe warp and the ground-truth
         rectification after fitting the best similarity. Isolates shape error
         and is not fooled by Adobe's own recentring, which is what made the
         old err_gt uninterpretable. Uncorrected baseline 98.4 px.
  fill   % of source pixels still inside the frame after the warp
  arc    circle-fit rms before -> after, plus fitted radius and conic aspect
         (1.000 = still a circle)

REFERENCE
  uncorrected : vdev 3.3-4.3, hdev 0.2-1.7, sim 98.4, fill 100, arc 0.76-1.00
  perfect     : vdev 0.00, hdev 0.00, sim 0.0, fill 86.8 (recentred truth),
                arc 0.000, aspect 1.000, f35 41.14

PER FAMILY

  (a) single wall — no break, and better than the geometry allows for
    One plane, so the wall's horizontals are parallel to the image plane, the
    horizontal vanishing point is at infinity and yaw is unobservable. Adobe
    still recovered most of it (residual yaw 0.36 deg Auto, 0.20 Full, against
    6 deg injected) off the weak convergence of the horizontals, and drove
    pitch to 0.14-0.35 deg. sim 13.0 / 6.8. This is the family most likely to
    collapse to identity and it did not.
    Carry the caveat: 6 deg is small. A frontal wall at 20-30 deg yaw has
    horizontals that no longer converge usefully and the yaw channel should
    die — untested here.

  (b) wall plus railings — no break, weakest Auto cell
    The railings restored the second vanishing point and Full was the best
    result in the set (sim 4.3, hdev 0.03). Auto managed only 0.83 of the ideal
    (sim 16.9) but stayed fully sensible. The near-vertical posts were not
    mistaken for another vertical family.

  (c) strong curves — no break; the arc is protected
      arc            mode  res pre -> post  ratio  radius pre -> post  aspect
      wall arc 7 m   auto     1.00 -> 0.36   0.36   342 -> 359          1.011
      wall arc 7 m   full     1.00 -> 0.34   0.34   342 -> 360          1.009
      wall arc 5 m   auto     0.76 -> 0.26   0.34   244 -> 256          1.011
      wall arc 5 m   full     0.76 -> 0.25   0.32   244 -> 257          1.009

      column ring 10 auto    26.35 -> 25.77  0.98   109 -> 120          n/a
      ideal (recentred truth)      0.00             350                1.000

    The wall arcs are in-plane circles, so a correct rectification must return
    them to circles. The conic fit says it does: aspect 1.009-1.011 against
    1.000 ideal (1.03 before), residual down 2.8-3.1x, radius moving 5% toward
    the true 350 rather than away. A straightness penalty that had flattened
    the arc would push the radius to thousands of px; it stays at 360, and the
    residual ellipticity (1.1%) matches the scene's global anisotropy (1.01),
    so all deformation is explained by the warp and none is arc-specific.
    The column rings are 3-D circles seen obliquely, so they fit a circle badly
    by construction (rms ~40 px before and after, ratio 0.98-1.01) and carry
    little signal; the wall arcs are the load-bearing test.

  (d) three planes — no break, but not informative
    Effectively the Manhattan case: sim 11.2 / 7.1 against 12.7 / 8.2 for
    CT_manhattan2 at the same pose. The third plane adds nothing the two-wall
    corner lacks.

CONTROLS — the failure mode is pitch, not structure
  B_strong Auto is the only WRONG cell and it is Manhattan: at 34 deg pitch /
  12 deg yaw Auto applies essentially none of the correction (sim 196.5 against
  a 195.8 baseline, frac 0.00, verticals worse after, 3.28 -> 4.26 deg, residual
  pitch 24.2 deg). Its Full is fine (19.9, 0.95). A_clean Auto is half-hearted
  (0.66, residual pitch 7.1 deg); Full is clean. So the Auto under-correction
  the earlier experiments saw is driven by correction magnitude, not by
  Manhattan-ness — at 15/6 Auto already reaches 0.83-0.91 of the ideal in every
  family, Manhattan control included.

FOCAL
  f35 solved 33.94-39.77 against a true 41.14 (17.5% low on three families,
  3.3% low on the railing). Full never re-estimated it — every Auto/Full pair
  shares one focal, independently confirming the 22:35 note that one shared
  analysis is cached into all slots and the modes only differ in the damping
  applied afterwards. Consequence: the sim-to-truth residual carries an aspect
  term, the arc conic comes out 1.1% elliptical, radii run 3-10% long. It is a
  uniform mild stretch, not a wrong rotation, and it is equally present in the
  Manhattan control (1.01 anisotropy), so it belongs to the focal estimator, not
  to non-Manhattan content.

WHICH FAMILIES BREAK
  None. Ranked by residual difficulty, worst first:
    (b) railing    — Auto only 0.83 of ideal, still sensible
    (a) single wall — yaw only weakly observable; handled at 6 deg, expected to
        fail at large yaw
    (d) three planes — indistinguishable from the Manhattan control
    (c) curves     — best protected; arcs survive at 0.34 px rms, aspect 1.009

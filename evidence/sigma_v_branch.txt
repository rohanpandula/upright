SIGMA_V — PINNED, AND THE EARLIER SPREAD EXPLAINED (branch bimodality)

Dense pure-pitch sweep, 2..40 deg step 2, 2 seeds (40 solves, all completed).
Naive pooled fit gave sigma_v = 19.77 +- 2.92 and a badly non-monotonic curve:
  34 deg 0.745 | 36 deg 0.053 | 38 deg 0.388 | 40 deg 0.354

PER-SEED INSPECTION shows it is not noise. At high pitch the solver is BIMODAL,
with two distinct solution branches that track the FOCAL ESTIMATE:

  img     orig   corr  frac   |H-I|    f35    branch
  V36S1  23.90  22.65  0.052  0.0229  40.08   weak / focal near truth
  V36S2  23.90  22.59  0.055  0.0241  39.91   weak / focal near truth
  V38S1  24.99  24.47  0.021  0.0098  39.99   weak

f35 > 32 pairs with WEAK correction; f35 <= 32 pairs with STRONG correction.
True focal is 41.14 mm, so the short-focal branch is the estimator failing and
the resulting over-correction following from it. Branch flips occur at 18, 20,
22, 28, 30, 38 and 40 deg; both seeds agree at 32 and 36.

CONCLUSION
sigma_v = 14.58 deg (CI 11.8-17.3) on the physically sane branch, which BRACKETS
the binary literal 0.2618 rad = 15.00 deg. The earlier 11.7-15.5 deg spread was
an artefact of pooling across branches, not measurement error.

CAVEATS, stated plainly
- The correct-focal fit has max residual 0.473, so sigma is well determined but
  the lambda/(lambda+1) SHAPE is only approximate. Do not present the shape as
  exact; the constant is the trustworthy part.
- L0 comes out ~3.5, well below the 12.0 used in upright_matched.py. The ported
  Auto is therefore too aggressive, consistent with the known 40% over-correction.
- n=18 across 2 seeds is thin for the good branch; 36 and 38 deg disagree in
  branch membership between seeds, so the branch assignment itself is stochastic.
